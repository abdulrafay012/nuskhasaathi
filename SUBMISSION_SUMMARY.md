# Product Summary — 1,371 characters

*Paste-ready for the hackathon submission form (limit 1,400).*

---

NuskhaSaathi turns a photographed handwritten prescription into spoken Urdu instructions.

In Pakistan, prescriptions carry English drug names and Latin shorthand — BD, TDS, 5/7 — in handwriting meant for a pharmacist. For an adult who cannot read English, that paper holds no information. Doses get taken twice a day instead of three, courses abandoned when the fever breaks, and dangerous drug pairs combined because two doctors never spoke.

The patient photographs the nuskha. Qwen-VL Plus reads the handwriting; Qwen Plus structures it into medicines with dosage, frequency and duration, badging each Confident or Uncertain; every medicine gets a plain-Urdu explanation read aloud. A deterministic table of 15 verified dangerous pairs — checked across all of a patient's prescriptions, not just one — raises an immediate warning.

Built beyond the demo: a daily dose schedule that routes unreadable timing to "confirm with your doctor" rather than guessing; patient correction that re-runs the safety check; a revocable caregiver link family can open with no account; an Urdu PDF; and voice Q&A grounded in that prescription.

It explains, never diagnoses. 21 endpoints, 118 passing tests, and a real accuracy benchmark whose limits we state openly.

Telemedicine connects you to a doctor. NuskhaSaathi makes sure what the doctor already wrote is understood.
